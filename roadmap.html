30-Day Technical Architect Interview Roadmap

Use the filters to focus the plan by week. Click each week heading to collapse or expand.

Week 1: Architecture Fundamentals

Build the foundation for NFRs, scale, data, distributed systems, caching, messaging, and first mock design.
Collapse

Day 1 Architecture mindset

Study functional vs NFR, scalability, availability, reliability, maintainability, and cost. Assignment: design a multi-tenant SaaS platform.

Day 2 Scaling basics

Master load balancing, horizontal scaling, vertical scaling, caching, CDN, and database scaling. Assignment: design for 1 million users and 100K concurrent users.

Day 3 Databases

Study SQL vs NoSQL, indexing, partitioning, replication, sharding, read replicas, and consistency. Explain SQL Server vs PostgreSQL vs MongoDB vs Cosmos DB.

Day 4 Distributed systems

Study CAP, consistency, availability, eventual consistency, and distributed transactions. Explain Order -> Payment -> Inventory -> Notification.

Day 5 Caching

Study cache-aside, write-through, write-behind, TTL, invalidation, cache stampede, and distributed cache failure. Use your Redis outage as an interview case.

Day 6 Messaging

Study RabbitMQ, Kafka, Azure Service Bus, queues vs topics, retry, DLQ, and idempotency.

Day 7 Mock interview

Design an Amazon-like order system in 45 minutes without Google.

Week 2: Cloud + Distributed Architecture

Move from concepts into Azure, AWS mapping, Kubernetes, security, observability, and resilience.
Collapse

Day 8 Azure architecture

Study App Service, Functions, AKS, Service Bus, Event Grid, Cosmos DB, Azure SQL, Key Vault, and API Management.

Day 9 AWS equivalent architecture

Map App Service to Elastic Beanstalk / ECS, Functions to Lambda, Service Bus to SQS / SNS, Cosmos DB to DynamoDB, Blob Storage to S3, AKS to EKS, Azure SQL to RDS, and Key Vault to Secrets Manager.

Day 10 Docker + Kubernetes

Explain why Kubernetes exists without saying only that it provides scalability.

Day 11 Security architecture

Study OAuth2, OpenID Connect, JWT, Entra ID, API gateway, RBAC, secrets, encryption, and zero trust.

Day 12 Observability

Study logs, metrics, traces, correlation IDs, OpenTelemetry, Application Insights, and alerting.

Day 13 Resilience

Study timeout, retry, exponential backoff, circuit breaker, bulkhead, rate limiting, and idempotency.

Day 14 Mock payment platform

Include 99.99% availability, retries, duplicate requests, payment failure, database failure, queue failure, and observability.

Week 3: Architecture Thinking

The most important week: DDD, CQRS, events, microservices trade-offs, and executive communication.
Collapse

Day 15 DDD

Study bounded context, aggregate, entity, value object, and domain event.

Day 16 CQRS

Do not memorize definitions. Answer: why would I not use CQRS?

Day 17 Event-driven architecture

Design Order -> Payment -> Inventory -> Shipping -> Notification.

Day 18 Microservices

Answer: when should you not use microservices? If you cannot answer convincingly, you are not ready to sell yourself as an architect.

Day 19 Modular monolith vs microservices

Make this one of your strongest interview topics.

Day 20 Architecture trade-offs

For every major technology, create Option A, Option B, pros, cons, cost, complexity, scale, and recommendation.

Day 21 Architecture presentation

Take one architecture and explain it in 10 minutes to a CTO, Product Manager, and Engineering Manager. Avoid jargon unless necessary.

Week 4: Interview Simulation

Practice difficult system design, production incidents, governance, behavioral stories, and a final mock exam.
Collapse

Day 22 Netflix-like video platform

Complete a full system design with requirements, NFRs, trade-offs, and failure modes.

Day 23 Uber-like ride platform

Focus on geo, matching, availability, location updates, and surge load.

Day 24 WhatsApp-like messaging system

Focus on messaging, delivery guarantees, fanout, presence, storage, and offline handling.

Day 25 Multi-tenant enterprise SaaS

This is particularly relevant to your experience. Cover tenancy, authorization, scale, reporting, integrations, and future AI.

Day 26 Production incident interview

Redis goes down, returns, and 1 million requests hit SQL. Explain what happens and how you prevent database collapse.

Day 27 Architecture governance

Study ADR, HLD, LLD, architecture review, standards, security review, technical debt, and governance.

Day 28 Behavioral Architect interview

Prepare STAR stories for architecture decision, production failure, developer conflict, stakeholder conflict, performance problem, migration, mentoring, difficult deadline, technical debt, and failed decision.

Day 29 Full mock interview

Run a 90-minute Architect mock interview with no notes.

The Assignment That Will Actually Judge You

The goal is not trivia. The goal is to expose how you think, decide, defend, and communicate.

Architect Assessment 1: Multi-tenant enterprise SaaS platform

Design for 10,000 companies, 1 million users, 100,000 concurrent users, 5,000 requests/sec peak, 99.9% availability, tenant isolation, authentication, RBAC, audit logging, notifications, reporting, file uploads, background jobs, third-party integrations, mobile and web clients, SQL transactional data, analytics/reporting workload, Azure deployment, and future AI features.

Your output

  • 1. Architecture diagram
  • 2. Major components
  • 3. Database strategy
  • 4. Tenant isolation strategy
  • 5. Authentication strategy
  • 6. API architecture
  • 7. Cache strategy
  • 8. Messaging strategy
  • 9. Background processing
  • 10. File storage
  • 11. Reporting architecture
  • 12. Scaling strategy
  • 13. Failure handling
  • 14. Security
  • 15. Observability
  • 16. CI/CD
  • 17. Disaster recovery
  • 18. Cost considerations
  • 19. Top 5 risks
  • 20. Alternatives you rejected
Most important: for every major decision, answer why and why not the alternative.

Assignment 2: Architecture Defense

After Assignment 1, your architecture will be attacked. You must defend your decisions under pressure.

  • Redis is down.
  • SQL is at 95% CPU.
  • One tenant consumes 70% of system capacity.
  • RabbitMQ is unavailable.
  • Azure region goes down.
  • A developer proposes replacing RabbitMQ with Kafka.
  • Product wants 10x traffic in 6 months.
  • CFO says the architecture is too expensive.
  • Security rejects the authentication model.

Assignment 3: Executive Thinking

Explain the same architecture in 5 minutes to a non-technical CEO. Avoid terms like CQRS, CAP, Kubernetes, Kafka, Redis, and DDD unless absolutely necessary.

  • What are we building?
  • Why this architecture?
  • What will it cost?
  • What risks exist?
  • How quickly can we scale?
  • What happens when something fails?
